# workbench outcome review checklist

Use this before claiming that a training, pilot, workbench, or agent workflow created durable value.

## original promise

- [ ] Original goal or acceptance criteria are linked or summarized.
- [ ] The expected user/business outcome is stated without tool hype.
- [ ] Original constraints, permissions, risks, and review gates are noted.

## current architecture

- [ ] Inputs, source material, and freshness expectations are identified.
- [ ] Workflow stages are mapped as files, scripts, tools, model calls, and human checks.
- [ ] Outputs and review surfaces are named.
- [ ] Owners or maintainers are named by role.
- [ ] Failure modes and manual fallback paths are listed.

## outcome evidence

- [ ] Direct outcome evidence is present, not only activity counts.
- [ ] Follow-on builds or second-order effects are captured when they exist.
- [ ] Adoption evidence says who used the workflow, how often, and what behavior changed.
- [ ] Negative evidence, blockers, and non-use are included.
- [ ] Evidence is source-linked enough for a reviewer to inspect.

## reusable learning

- [ ] Misunderstood terms or missing definitions are recorded.
- [ ] Missing templates, examples, scripts, checks, or interface affordances are named.
- [ ] Tacit operator judgment is either encoded or explicitly left as a human review point.
- [ ] The next change is routed to a template, mutable workbench, archive, backlog, or stop decision.

## closeout

- [ ] One owner and one next verification are assigned.
- [ ] The review produced or updated at least one durable artifact.
- [ ] A fresh operator can explain what changed without reading the original chat or meeting transcript.
