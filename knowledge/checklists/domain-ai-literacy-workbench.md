# domain AI literacy workbench checklist

Use this before claiming a team, class, profession, or community is ready to use
AI responsibly in a domain workflow.

## scope

- [ ] The domain outcome is stated in workflow terms, not tool terms.
- [ ] Participant roles are named without exposing private identities.
- [ ] The decision owner and reviewer roles are explicit.
- [ ] The workbench states what is out of scope.

## literacy before tool use

- [ ] The program teaches stable primitives before vendor features.
- [ ] Participants see examples of both useful and harmful AI use.
- [ ] The training names where AI uncertainty, hallucination, privacy, or review
      risk enters the workflow.
- [ ] The program explains when simpler scripts, templates, or manual review are
      preferable to AI.

## use-boundary map

- [ ] Human-only tasks are recorded.
- [ ] AI-assisted tasks are recorded.
- [ ] Review-required tasks are recorded with reviewer role.
- [ ] Automatable deterministic tasks are separated from model/agent tasks.
- [ ] Prohibited tasks are recorded with rationale.
- [ ] Boundary disagreements and unknowns are visible.

## fundamentals and judgment

- [ ] The workbench names fundamentals practitioners must still learn or practice
      without AI.
- [ ] AI outputs require practitioners to explain or defend accepted artifacts
      where domain accountability matters.
- [ ] Review criteria are concrete enough to reject polished but wrong output.
- [ ] Novices are not allowed to outsource the exact practice needed to develop
      later judgment.

## community/workbench operation

- [ ] Repeated workflows have rooms or local routing notes.
- [ ] Assistant roles are tied to concrete artifacts or review functions.
- [ ] Conversation decisions are extracted into files or ledgers.
- [ ] Raw discussions, private identities, and sensitive source material have a
      consent and retention rule.
- [ ] A fresh participant can find the current boundaries and examples without
      replaying old chats.

## evidence

- [ ] At least one before/after example exists for a real workflow.
- [ ] Generated artifacts link to sources, review notes, and acceptance/rejection
      status.
- [ ] Implementation evidence shows a changed document, policy, workflow,
      decision, or eval case.
- [ ] The learning loop records failures, objections, and boundary updates.
- [ ] Outcome checks measure changed work, not only attendance or satisfaction.
