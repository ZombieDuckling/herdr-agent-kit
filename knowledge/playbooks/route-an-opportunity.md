# route a paid or high-priority opportunity

Use this before turning a community request, internal complaint, lead, or repeated service task into learning, delegation, automation, or software.

## output

Produce an opportunity-routing note with:

- buyer/user or accountable requester role;
- painful job and current cost in time, risk, delay, quality, or missed revenue;
- source inputs and data boundary;
- acceptance test and review owner role;
- available capacity and reusable assets;
- first manual or semi-manual delivery path;
- next escalation trigger.

## procedure

1. **Capture the pain in the requester's words.** Do not translate it into a tool or agent idea yet.
2. **Qualify value.** Record who pays, who uses, what happens if nothing changes, and what proof would count as accepted.
3. **Map fulfillment capacity.** Identify the operator role, existing asset, template, checklist, or script that can produce the first useful output.
4. **Route the smallest attempt.** Assign one bounded experiment with source inputs, allowed actions, due artifact, and review owner.
5. **Deliver in a workbench.** Keep request slip, sources, working notes, output, checks, and handoff together.
6. **Record acceptance.** Mark accepted, rejected, revised, or blocked with reason and evidence.
7. **Compress repetition.** Only after repeated accepted requests, create or improve a template, script, model call, agent, integration, or product surface.

## qualification questions

- What request or complaint recurs without a good owner?
- What manual work is already being paid for, delayed, or avoided?
- What scarce operator time would this buy back?
- What reusable asset already exists?
- What proof would make the requester trust the result?
- What private details must stay out of shared notes and examples?

## stop conditions

Stop before building when:

- no real requester or accountable user exists;
- the requested output cannot be reviewed;
- available capacity cannot deliver even one manual version;
- the proposed software mostly routes hypothetical supply, not observed demand;
- the board would expose private deal, person, customer, channel, or financial details in a shared corpus.
