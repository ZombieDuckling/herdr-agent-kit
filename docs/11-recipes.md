# Recipes

Things to say to your lead. Each one triggers the skills that matter; you do not need to name them.

## Websites

> Build me a one-page site for my bakery, Crumb & Co, in Cape Town. Here are the photos: ~/Pictures/crumb.
> Keep it warm and simple. Don't publish it; show me locally.

The lead plans the pages, has workers build them, generates any missing art, checks it running (web-qa),
and gives you a local link with the numbers.

> Give the site at ~/Projects/shop a UI upgrade. Same brand and content. Use their photos. Don't push.

> Score my site like an Awwwards jury and list the ten biggest fixes. *(needs the design pack)*

## Code

> The tests in ~/Projects/api fail since yesterday. Find out why and fix it. Show me the diff before you commit.

> Audit ~/Projects/app for bugs and security problems. Use real scanners. Report numbers, fix the defects, leave house-style findings.

> Take GitHub issue #42 in my repo to a pull request. Don't merge.

## Research and writing

> Write me a researched report on heat pumps for a small house in a cold climate, with citations I can check.

> Explain how our deploy works in plain words for a new team member.

## Making things

> Design a wall bracket for a 30 mm pipe that I can 3D print, and give me the STL.

## Running the team

> Spin up workers for this; use free ones wherever you can, and check their work.

> What's running, and what is each worker doing?

> We're near my weekly limit. Hand this workspace to an OpenCode lead.

> Turn what we just did into a skill so you do it the same way next time.

> Add to my house rules: never use stock photos.

## Your own projects

> This is my project at ~/Projects/shop. Business questions go to the owner by email. Deploy is a push to
> main. Remember that.

The lead saves it to memory (and, if you want, to the "My projects" section of your house rules).
